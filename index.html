      How the score works

      The score measures how much a song leans on singing the same lines again and again. It is worked out in three steps:

      1. Count each unique line. For every line that appears in the song, count how many times it is sung, including repeats called for by directions like “Chorus” or “(x2)”.
      2. Square each count. A line sung once gives 1; twice gives 4; five times gives 25. Squaring makes heavy repetition weigh far more than light repetition.
      3. Average the squares. Add the squared counts and divide by the number of unique lines.

      score = (sum of each unique line's count²) ÷ (number of unique lines)

      Examples.

      • No line is ever repeated: every count is 1, so the score is 1, the lowest possible.
      • Four unique lines, each sung twice: (4 + 4 + 4 + 4) ÷ 4 = 4.
      • Ten unique lines, nine sung once and one sung ten times: (9 × 1 + 100) ÷ 10 = 10.9. A single line hammered over and over pushes the score up sharply.

      Lines match when their words match. Punctuation, capital letters, extra spaces, curly or straight quotes, and verse numbers at the start of a line are ignored.

      The psalm figures use the ESV text, one poetic line per line. The hymn figures use the hymn texts in Let's Sing.

      Privacy

      Privacy. Song Repetition Visualizer uses Google Analytics to count visits and see which features get used, so the app can be improved. You don't need an account, and nothing you type into the app is collected. Your data will never be sold or given away.
